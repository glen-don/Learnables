Best Apps for Learning European Portuguese in 2026

March 10, 2026

If you are learning Portuguese for Portugal, you already know the frustration. You download a highly rated language app, spend weeks building a streak, and then land in Lisbon only to realize you have been learning Brazilian Portuguese the entire time. The pronunciation is different. The grammar is different. Even everyday vocabulary is different. And yes, Portuguese people will notice.

European Portuguese (PT-PT) is one of the most underserved languages in the app world. The vast majority of "Portuguese" courses actually teach Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR), which is about as helpful for life in Portugal as learning American English would be for passing a Scottish dialect exam. Same language family, very different sound.

We tested every major language app that claims to offer Portuguese and evaluated each one specifically for European Portuguese learners. Here is what we found.

Why European Portuguese Is So Hard to Find in Apps

Brazil has roughly 215 million people. Portugal has about 10 million. From a business perspective, app developers build for the bigger market. That means Brazilian Portuguese dominates every major platform, and European Portuguese gets treated as an afterthought, if it gets included at all.

The problem is not just accent. European Portuguese has distinct grammar patterns (like the use of "tu" conjugations instead of "voce"), different vocabulary for everyday items (autocarro vs. onibus, telemavel vs. celular, pequeno almoco vs. cafe da manha), and a pronunciation style that swallows vowels and blends consonants in ways Brazilian Portuguese does not. If you learn Brazilian pronunciation, you will struggle to understand native Portuguese speakers in Lisbon, Porto, or the Algarve.

So which apps actually support the Portuguese you need? Let us go through them one by one.

1. Duolingo

European Portuguese support: None

This is the one that catches most people off guard. Duolingo is the world's most popular language learning app, with over 100 million monthly users. Its Portuguese course is extensive, well-structured, and completely Brazilian.

There is no European Portuguese option. No toggle. No setting. If you select "Portuguese" on Duolingo, you are getting Brazilian Portuguese, period. Duolingo has acknowledged requests for a PT-PT course but has shown no signs of building one.

The real-world consequences are not trivial. Learners who study on Duolingo for months and then move to Portugal report being laughed at, corrected constantly, or simply not understood. The pronunciation patterns are so different that Portuguese people often switch to English rather than try to parse Brazilian-accented Portuguese from a foreigner.

Duolingo is a solid app for many languages, and it works fine if your goal is Brazilian Portuguese. But for European Portuguese, it is a dead end. If you are looking for an alternative to Duolingo for Portuguese, you will need to look elsewhere.

Price: Free with ads, $7.99/month for Super Duolingo

2. Babbel

European Portuguese support: Limited

Babbel deserves credit for at least acknowledging that European Portuguese exists. Their Portuguese course is built around PT-PT rather than PT-BR, which already makes it better than Duolingo for Portugal-bound learners.

The problem is depth. Babbel's Portuguese content is one of its thinnest language offerings. You get the basics: greetings, ordering food, asking for directions. But the course runs out of material relatively quickly compared to their Spanish or French programs. Once you finish the beginner and lower-intermediate modules, there is not much left.

The teaching method is also drill-based, which means you are translating isolated sentences and filling in blanks. This can help with vocabulary memorization but does very little for reading comprehension or listening skills in context. You will not encounter full paragraphs or stories.

Babbel is a reasonable starting point for absolute beginners in European Portuguese, but you will outgrow it fast.

Price: $14.95/month, or $6.95/month on an annual plan

3. Beelinguapp

European Portuguese support: Partial (with issues)

Beelinguapp pioneered the bilingual reading concept, displaying text in two languages side by side while audio plays. In theory, this is exactly what European Portuguese learners need: real stories with real audio to train your ear and build reading skills simultaneously.

In practice, Beelinguapp has significant problems. The app uses AI-generated voices rather than native speakers, and the pronunciation is frequently wrong. For a language like European Portuguese, where correct pronunciation is already hard to find in learning materials, AI voices that mispronounce words are actively harmful. You will internalize incorrect sounds.

Users also report buggy downloads, stories that crash mid-read, and translations that are flat-out wrong. One notorious example is "flan leather" as a translation for "leche flan." These are not minor issues when you are relying on the app to teach you a language accurately.

The European Portuguese content is also limited, with most of the library focused on more popular language pairs. If you are curious about the bilingual reading approach but want something more polished, read our full comparison of Beelinguapp alternatives.

Price: Free with ads, $4.17/month on an annual plan

4. LingQ

European Portuguese support: Yes, user-generated

LingQ takes a different approach entirely. Instead of curated courses, it gives you a platform to import and read any text in your target language. You can import news articles, blog posts, book chapters, or podcast transcripts and read them with integrated dictionary lookups and word tracking.

For European Portuguese, this means you can theoretically access unlimited content. The problem is that "theoretically" is doing a lot of heavy lifting in that sentence.

LingQ's interface is, to put it kindly, dated. The app feels like it was designed in 2012 and has not been meaningfully updated since. Navigation is confusing. The reading experience is cluttered with buttons, counters, and statistics that distract from the actual content. For a tool built around reading, the reading experience is surprisingly poor.

The built-in European Portuguese content is mostly user-generated, which means quality varies wildly. Some lessons have native audio. Others do not. Some translations are accurate. Others are machine-generated. You spend as much time evaluating content quality as you do actually learning.

LingQ also comes with a steep price tag. At $12.99 per month, it is one of the most expensive language apps on the market, and the premium tier runs even higher. For advanced learners who already know what content they want to import and do not mind an ugly interface, LingQ can work. For everyone else, it is frustrating.

Price: $12.99/month, or $8.33/month annually

5. Practice Portuguese

European Portuguese support: Yes, dedicated

Practice Portuguese is the passion project of two people who clearly love European Portuguese. It is one of the only resources built exclusively for PT-PT learners, with native audio, cultural notes, and a genuine focus on how Portuguese is actually spoken in Portugal.

The content quality is high. Their podcast-style lessons and "shorties" (short dialogues with transcripts) are well-produced and genuinely useful. The team records everything with native European Portuguese speakers, and you can hear the difference immediately compared to AI-generated alternatives.

The catch? Practice Portuguese is web-only. There is no mobile app. In 2026, that is a significant limitation. If you want to study on your commute, in a cafe, or anywhere without a laptop open, you are out of luck. The web interface also feels more like a blog with embedded audio players than a structured learning platform.

Practice Portuguese is an excellent supplementary resource, especially for intermediate learners. But as a primary learning tool, the lack of a mobile app and structured progression makes it hard to recommend as your only resource.

Price: Free content available, $15/month for full access

6. Learnables

European Portuguese support: Yes, full native audio

Learnables takes the bilingual story concept and builds it properly for European Portuguese. Every story is narrated by native European Portuguese speakers with word-level audio synchronization, so you can hear exactly how each word sounds as you read it. Tap any word to see its translation instantly.

The app is designed around comprehensible input, the idea that you learn a language most effectively by reading and listening to material you can mostly understand, with just enough challenge to push your skills forward. Stories are graded by difficulty, so you start with simple narratives and progress to more complex content as your reading ability improves.

Unlike Beelinguapp, the audio comes from real human speakers, not AI. Unlike LingQ, the interface is clean and the content is curated for quality. Unlike Practice Portuguese, it is a proper mobile app available on both iOS and Android. And unlike Duolingo and Babbel, it focuses on building real reading comprehension through full stories rather than isolated sentence drills.

The free tier gives you 3 pages per day, which is enough to build a daily reading habit. The premium plan unlocks unlimited reading for $5.99 per month, making it one of the most affordable options on this list.

If you want to learn the Portuguese that people actually speak in Portugal, and you want to do it through reading rather than multiple-choice quizzes, Learnables is built specifically for you.

Price: Free (3 pages/day), $5.99/month for unlimited

So Which App Should You Choose?

If your goal is European Portuguese, your options are genuinely limited, and it helps to be honest about that.

Skip Duolingo entirely if you are learning for Portugal. It does not offer European Portuguese and there are no signs that it will.

Use Babbel if you want a structured beginner course and do not mind that it ends quickly. It is a fine starting point, just not a complete solution.

Avoid Beelinguapp for European Portuguese specifically. The AI voices will teach you incorrect pronunciation, and the translation errors undermine the entire bilingual reading experience.

Consider LingQ only if you are an advanced learner comfortable importing your own content and tolerating a rough interface. It is powerful but not user-friendly.

Bookmark Practice Portuguese as a supplementary resource. The content is excellent, but the lack of a mobile app limits how and when you can use it.

Try Learnables if you want the best combination of proper European Portuguese audio, a reading-based learning method, clean mobile experience, and affordable pricing. The free tier lets you test it without any commitment.

The good news is that the European Portuguese app landscape is improving. The bad news is that it has been neglected for years, and most mainstream apps still have not caught up. Choose your tools carefully, and make sure the Portuguese you are learning is actually the Portuguese you need.
